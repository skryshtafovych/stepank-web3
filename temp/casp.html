CASP15 (2022) showed enormous progress in modeling multimolecular protein complexes. The assembly modeling has been assessed in CASP since 2016. After the success of AlphaFold2 in CASP14, it was expected that deep learning methodology would be extended to multimeric modeling. Indeed, CASP15 showed that newly developed methods are capable of accurately reproducing structures of oligomeric complexes and outperform CASP14 methods by a large margin.

Models based on templates identified by sequence similarity remain the most accurate. Over the course of the CASP experiments there have been enormous improvements in this area, with a new burst of progress in CASP12 (2016). CASP14 marked an extraordinary increase in accuracy with the emergence of AlphaFold2.

Modeling proteins with no similarity to existing structures (ab initio) is the most challenging task. CASP has witnessed sustained progress, especially for small proteins. CASP13 and CASP14 saw substantial improvements due to advanced deep learning techniques, with AlphaFold2 models now competitive with experimental accuracy.

The most notable progress in recent CASPs resulted from sustained improvement in methods for predicting contacts between residues. Average precision doubled from CASP11 to CASP12, and CASP13 registered another leap in accuracy. This theoretical advance has led to improved accuracy of 3D models.

In early CASPs, models occasionally helped solve structures, but these were exceptions. In CASP14, four structures were solved with the aid of AlphaFold2 models, demonstrating the power of new methods for all classes of modeling difficulty. A detailed account is provided in the Proteins paper by Kryshtafovych et al, 2021.

The refinement category assesses the ability of methods to improve available models. CASP assessments show two trends: some molecular dynamics methods offer modest, consistent improvements, while more aggressive methods can provide substantial improvements, though sometimes at the price of consistency. The examples show notable refinements from CASP12.

Data-assisted modeling, where low-resolution experimental data are combined with computational methods, is increasingly important. CASP experiments have included a special sub-category for this. Examples from CASP12 demonstrate the increased accuracy of assisted predictions compared to non-assisted models from the same predictor.

Welcome to the Protein Structure Prediction Center!

Our goal is to help advance the methods of identifying protein structure from sequence. The Center provides objective testing of these methods via blind prediction in the Critical Assessment of protein Structure Prediction (CASP) experiments.

There have been fifteen previous CASP experiments, with the sixteenth planned for May 2024. You can access descriptions and full data via the navigation menu.

Details of the experiments are published in the journal Proteins: Structure, Function and Bioinformatics. The CASP proceedings include papers on the experiments, evaluation measures, and reports from assessment teams.
